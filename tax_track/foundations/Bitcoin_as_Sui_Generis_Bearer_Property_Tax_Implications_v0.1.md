## 2. Core Thesis for Tax Treatment

Bitcoin, particularly in self-custodied form, constitutes **sui generis bearer property** and an **adjacent currency**.

- It has no issuer, no central control point, and no promoter.  
- Its scarcity is produced by irreversible energy expenditure (proof-of-work) and is enforced by global, permissionless consensus.  
- Ultimate control can reside entirely as memorised knowledge (BIP-39 seed phrases) or through decentralised multi-party cryptographic structures.  
- It requires no dedicated state infrastructure or intermediary for its existence, security, or peer-to-peer transfer.

Consequently, the simple acts of **acquiring, holding, self-custody transferring, or operating a node** do not, and should not, constitute taxable events or capital-control triggers.

Taxation remains coherent only when limited to **realised gains arising from voluntary economic activity** that has a sufficient territorial nexus to the taxing jurisdiction and that occurs at observable interfaces with the formal financial system. Mere holding, pure peer-to-peer movement, and self-custody fall outside the legitimate tax base.

This position is derived deductively from:

- Natural Law (self-ownership, homesteading via labour-mixing, non-aggression principle, privacy of thought);  
- Austrian economics (spontaneous order, sound money, impossibility of coherent taxation of the monetary medium itself); and  
- International law coordination principles analogous to Double Tax Agreements (allocation of taxing rights only over realised local economic activity).

## 3. Key Principles Applied to Taxation

### 3.1 Distinction Between the Asset and Economic Activity
Taxing the asset *qua* asset (unrealised appreciation, mere possession, or borderless movement) collapses into coercion at the human endpoint and lacks any corresponding unique state service. Taxing realised economic activity at identifiable interfaces remains within conventional and legitimate scope.

### 3.2 Self-Custody as Highest Fiduciary and Sovereign Standard
Self-custody or properly structured decentralised multi-party control is the highest expression of Bitcoin fiduciary responsibility for both natural and juristic persons. Regulatory frameworks should recognise this standard rather than treat it as a risk to be mitigated through compelled disclosure or intermediary mandates.

### 3.3 Separation from Issuer-Dependent Crypto Assets
Bitcoin is structurally distinct from the broader class of crypto assets (no pre-mine, immutable monetary policy, no discretionary governance, energy-secured consensus). Legislative and tax definitions must maintain this separation to avoid intellectual incoherence and the misapplication of intermediary-centric rules.

### 3.4 Temporary Protections During Regulatory Transition
Where rules are conflicting, unclear, or in transition, adequate protections are required: temporary amnesty or grace periods, clear safe harbours for good-faith actors, and non-retrospectivity. These measures prevent unjust outcomes while the legal framework adapts to technological reality.

### 3.5 Right to Sovereign Private Compute
The right to operate open-source Bitcoin software (including full nodes) on personal hardware is a fundamental extension of privacy, freedom of expression, and property. Tax or capital-flow rules that create pathways for compelled linkage of identity to private computation on personal devices engage constitutional protections.

## 4. Application of the Principles

The foundational position set out in this document supplies the principled basis for recommending, in every jurisdiction:

- Explicit recognition of self-custodied Bitcoin as *sui generis* bearer property / adjacent currency;
- Exclusion of mere holding, self-custody transfers, and node operation from taxable events and capital-control restrictions;
- Limitation of taxation to realised gains arising from voluntary economic activity that has a sufficient territorial nexus;
- Safe harbours, grace periods and non-retroactivity while classification remains unsettled or in transition;
- Protection of the Right to Sovereign Private Compute — the right to operate open-source Bitcoin software, including full nodes, on personal hardware without compelled identity linkage.

The institutional trajectory that produced the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) and related automatic-exchange instruments is documented in the supporting reference *Global_CARF_History_Precedents_and_Governing_Bodies.md*. That record confirms the consistent pattern of expanding intermediary reporting obligations while leaving pure self-custody outside the core automatic-exchange framework itself.

Nation-specific operational detail (local CARF implementation timelines, domestic court decisions, particular capital-flow manuals, comment windows, etc.) is maintained in dedicated jurisdiction status documents. This foundations document remains the controlling, jurisdiction-agnostic Tier-1 reference.

## 6. Truth & Clarity Summary

**Internal coherence**: High. The positions follow deductively from the published Natural Law + Austrian + international-law whitepapers.

**Alignment with current positive law**: Partial. Most jurisdictions continue to treat crypto assets as property subject to tax on disposal. The argument is normative and reformist; it supplies principled grounds for legislative and administrative evolution rather than unilateral non-compliance.

**Orientation**: This workstream constructs a freedom-oriented legislative discussion framework. Existing restrictive classifications are recorded accurately as the current state of the law; they do not constrain the normative recommendations developed herein. The methodology is designed for clean duplication across jurisdictions: global principles remain stable in this Tier-1 document, while local operational status is maintained in thin, jurisdiction-specific companions.
