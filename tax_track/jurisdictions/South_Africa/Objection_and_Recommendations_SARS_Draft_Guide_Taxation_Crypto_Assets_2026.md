## 1. Introduction and Standing

I submit this formal objection and set of recommendations in response to the *Draft Guide to the Taxation of Crypto Assets* published by the South African Revenue Service on 1 July 2026.

The Draft Guide continues the treatment of crypto assets as intangible assets / property and leaves the critical revenue-versus-capital distinction to general case law. While this approach is consistent with the current administrative practice of SARS, it fails to recognise the qualitatively distinct character of Bitcoin and thereby perpetuates legal uncertainty, enforcement friction, and unnecessary collision with constitutional rights.

This submission is grounded in the following publicly released works of the MoloBTC project (all under BSOL v1.0):

## 2. Core Objection

Bitcoin is **sui generis bearer property** and an **adjacent currency**. It is not interchangeable with the broader class of crypto assets.

- It has no issuer, no central control point, and no promoter.  
- Its scarcity is produced by irreversible energy expenditure and enforced by global, permissionless consensus.  
- Ultimate control can reside entirely as memorised knowledge (seed phrases) or through decentralised multi-party cryptographic structures.  
- It requires no dedicated state infrastructure or intermediary for its existence, security, or peer-to-peer transfer.

The Draft Guide’s undifferentiated treatment of “crypto assets” as a single category forces Bitcoin into legacy property boxes designed for issued or intermediary-dependent instruments. This creates fundamental contradictions with the protocol’s architecture and with the constitutional rights to property (section 25), privacy (section 14), and freedom of trade (section 22) of the Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, 1996.

In particular, the continued reliance on a facts-and-circumstances test for revenue versus capital treatment, without bright-line recognition of long-term self-custody or pure monetary holding, leaves ordinary holders exposed to retrospective re-characterisation risk and creates unnecessary compliance friction.

## 3. Specific Recommendations

### 3.1 Explicit Recognition of Bitcoin’s Distinct Character
The final Guide should contain a dedicated section recognising Bitcoin as a *sui generis* bearer asset and adjacent currency, distinct from other crypto assets that possess issuers, discretionary governance, or pre-mine structures.

### 3.2 Exclusion of Mere Holding and Self-Custody Transfers from Taxable Events
The Guide should state clearly that the mere acquisition, holding, or self-custody transfer of Bitcoin (including transfers between wallets under the common control of the same natural or juristic person, and the operation of personal full nodes) does not constitute a disposal or taxable event.

### 3.3 Limitation of Taxation to Realised Economic Activity with Territorial Nexus
Taxation should be confined to realised gains arising from voluntary economic activity that has a sufficient territorial nexus to the Republic. Pure peer-to-peer movement and self-custody should fall outside the tax base.

### 3.4 Safe Harbours and Transitional Protections
Where classification remains unsettled (particularly the revenue-versus-capital distinction and the ongoing High Court conflict on exchange-control characterisation), the Guide should provide:

- Clear safe harbours for individuals and entities acting in good faith;  
- Reasonable grace periods and non-retrospectivity for periods of regulatory uncertainty;  
- Explicit confirmation that self-custody and personal node operation do not, by themselves, give rise to reporting or tax obligations under CARF or the Income Tax Act.

### 3.5 Alignment with the Right to Sovereign Private Compute
The Guide should affirm that the operation of open-source Bitcoin software on personal hardware constitutes protected private computation and does not, without more, trigger tax or disclosure obligations.

## 4. Proposed Model Language

I recommend the insertion of the following (or substantially similar) language in the final Guide:

> “Bitcoin controlled through cryptographic means — including Bitcoin operated on nodes in private homes as part of the global decentralised network, self-custodied Bitcoin, and Bitcoin held in decentralised or multi-party control structures (whether by natural persons or juristic persons) — shall be recognised as a *sui generis* bearer asset and adjacent currency.  
>  
> The mere holding, cross-border movement, node operation, or control of such Bitcoin shall not constitute a taxable event. Taxation shall apply only to realised gains arising from voluntary economic activity with a sufficient territorial nexus to the Republic of South Africa.  
>  
> Adequate protections, including temporary amnesty and grace periods, shall apply where regulations or administrative guidance remain unclear or in transition.”

---

## 5. Conclusion

The *Draft Guide to the Taxation of Crypto Assets* presents an opportunity for SARS to move beyond undifferentiated property treatment and to recognise the distinct character of Bitcoin. Doing so would reduce legal uncertainty, protect constitutional rights, align South African practice with the economic and technical reality of the protocol, and provide a coherent foundation for future legislative development.

I urge SARS to incorporate the recommendations set out above and to treat Bitcoin as the *sui generis* bearer asset and adjacent currency that it is.
