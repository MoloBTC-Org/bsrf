## 1. Purpose

This document records the domestic operational status of the OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) in South Africa as implemented by the South African Revenue Service (SARS). It is a factual companion to the international institutional history set out in *Global_CARF_History_Precedents_and_Governing_Bodies.md*.

---

## 2. Implementation Status (as of August 2026)

SARS has incorporated CARF into South Africa’s domestic framework. Reporting obligations for Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Providers (RCASPs) with a South African nexus are live.

**Key timelines**

- Collection of reportable information began: **1 March 2026**
- First reporting period: **1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027**
- First returns due to SARS: **31 May 2027**
- First automatic exchange of information with other jurisdictions: **September 2027**

SARS has published the final External Business Requirement Specification (BRS v1.5) and aligned schemas (CARF_SARS and CARF_OECD).

---

## 3. Scope

**Who must report**  
Crypto exchanges, brokers, dealers, custodians, trading platforms, and entities facilitating crypto payments or transfers that have a South African nexus or serve South African tax residents. FSCA supervision does not exempt them from CARF reporting obligations.

**What is reported**  
Customer identification (name, tax residency, TIN, address, etc.), number of wallets, and aggregated transaction data in the standard CARF categories (crypto-to-crypto exchanges, fiat-to-crypto purchases, crypto-to-fiat disposals, wallet transfers, and certain large retail payments above the applicable threshold).

**Who is not a direct reporter**  
Individual self-custody users and personal node operators are **not** direct reporters under the framework, provided they are not operating as in-scope CASPs providing services to others.

---

## 4. Relationship to other South African regimes

CARF is a **tax transparency** measure administered by SARS. It sits alongside:

- Existing income-tax and capital-gains-tax obligations that continue to apply to individuals and entities on their own crypto transactions.
- The separate capital-flow / exchange-control track administered by SARB FinSurv (Draft Crypto Assets Manual for Cross-Border Activities, comment deadline 30 September 2026).
- FSCA conduct licensing and FIC AML/CFT obligations.

The SARB’s earlier draft Authorised Alternative Remittance Provider (ARP) regime explicitly excludes virtual assets and therefore has no direct bearing on Bitcoin channels.

---

## 5. Relevance to self-custody and *sui generis* analysis

CARF, consistent with its international design, targets regulated intermediaries. Pure self-custody activity and personal Bitcoin node operation that never interact with a Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider fall outside the direct reporting obligations of the framework. This boundary remains intact under the South African implementation.

Domestic rules layered on top of CARF (in particular any future interaction with the Draft Crypto Assets Manual’s treatment of non-custodial wallet transfers) may create additional friction at the intermediary interface; they do not convert pure self-custody itself into a reportable activity under CARF.
