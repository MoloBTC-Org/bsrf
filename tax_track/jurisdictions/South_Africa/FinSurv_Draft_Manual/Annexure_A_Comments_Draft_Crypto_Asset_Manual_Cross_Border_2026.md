These written comments are submitted in the format prescribed in Annexure A to Exchange Control Circular No. 19/2026: commentator particulars and a four-column matrix (Section / Paragraph / Proposed amendments / Motivation).

The comments implement one distinction throughout. A transfer between a domestic Authorised CASP and an offshore CASP, or a change of beneficial owner across the CMA border, is a capital-flow event. A resident taking Bitcoin onto their own keys inside the Republic is not. Where a resident already presents Bitcoin on the financial statements as held to meet short-term operating commitments — the same role cash and cash equivalents play — a conversion of that Bitcoin into Rand, or Rand into that Bitcoin, at a domestic Authorised CASP or Authorised Dealer is a change of working medium, not an export. Where the resident already presents Bitcoin as an investment, a sale into Rand at that same desk is a realisation. Whether that realisation is taxable is a question for the Income Tax Act and for SARS. These comments do not ask FinSurv to hide it. They ask that it happen onshore.

## A.1 Definitions — insertions

| Section | Paragraph | Proposed amendments | Motivation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A.1 | After “Authorised Dealer” | Bitcoin means the Bitcoin network and the units recorded on its public blockchain. Bitcoin has no issuer, no administrator and no person with authority to freeze, recall or inflate the unit. Its supply schedule and consensus rules are not alterable by the National Treasury, FinSurv or an Authorised CASP. Bitcoin is distinct from an issued crypto asset. | The Manual treats every digital token as one class. Without a defined term, rules written for issued tokens are applied to a bearer protocol they do not describe. The same distinction was put to Treasury on 30 June 2026 and to SARS on 31 August 2026. |
| A.1 | After “Bitcoin” | Sui generis bearer property means Bitcoin under the exclusive cryptographic control of a natural or juristic person, where title follows control of the private key or seed material, and where no Authorised CASP maintains an account relationship in respect of that unit. | Self-custodied Bitcoin is not an account, not an issued liability and not a security. |
| A.1 | After the above | Adjacent currency means Bitcoin functioning as a medium of exchange or store of value alongside legal tender. It is not Rand, is not foreign currency as defined in this Manual, and is not an issued e-money or stable-value token. Recognition as an adjacent currency does not confer legal-tender status. | A.1 already excludes crypto assets from “foreign currency.” Adjacent currency records that exclusion in positive terms so a key movement is not treated as an Authorised Dealer FX export. It describes the protocol’s public-digital-cash function beside the Rand. It does not invent a tax election. |
| A.1 | After “Resident temporarily abroad” | Self-custody means control of Bitcoin solely by a natural or juristic person through private keys, seed material, or a multi-signature or multi-party arrangement in which no Authorised CASP holds a unilateral spending key. | The Manual defines the wallet type but not the legal relationship. Self-custody is that relationship. It is not, without more, an offshore account or a CASP product. |
| A.1 | After “Self-custody” | Self-custody transfer means a transfer of Bitcoin between wallets under the common beneficial control of the same person. It includes a withdrawal from a South African custodial wallet hosted by an Authorised CASP to that person’s own non-custodial wallet where no offshore CASP participates and beneficial ownership does not change. | This is the movement Table 1 presently classifies as an export of capital. Defining it as a change of custody, not of owner or jurisdiction, is the precondition for correcting B.1(A)(ii) and Table 1. |
| A.1 | After “Self-custody transfer” | Domestic self-custody event means a self-custody transfer effected by a resident while the receiving wallet remains under that resident’s control. It is not an export or import of capital and is not a cross-border crypto asset transaction merely because the wallet is non-custodial. | A resident who bought Bitcoin from a South African CASP and withdraws it to their own keys has not taken capital out of the Republic. |
| A.1 | Alphabetic point | Realisation means the conversion into Rand or another fiat currency, through an Authorised CASP or an Authorised Dealer, of Bitcoin that the resident already presents as an investment or other non-operating holding. Onshore receipt means the taking up, on the financial statements of a resident, of Bitcoin or Rand proceeds that represent (a) consideration for a South African-source business supply or (b) a return of or on an investment previously externalised in an approved manner. A realisation into Rand is a visible event at which the Income Tax Act may impose tax. This Manual should not prevent that event from occurring onshore. | Recognised proceeds must be able to come home. Blocking inward movement from own keys produces neither a FinSurv statistic nor taxable Rand. |
| A.1 | After “Realisation” | Working-medium Bitcoin means Bitcoin that the resident already presents, under the financial reporting framework it applies (IFRS, IFRS for SMEs, or South African GAAP), as held to meet short-term operating commitments rather than as an investment — the same role cash and cash equivalents play. This Manual does not reclassify that Bitcoin as legal tender, as foreign currency, or as cash for the purposes of IAS 7. | FinSurv follows the purpose already on the face of the accounts. Commentators do not invent an election. The IFRS Interpretations Committee’s view that a cryptocurrency holding is not IAS 7 cash is left untouched. |
| A.1 | After “Working-medium Bitcoin” | Investment holding means Bitcoin that the resident already presents as an investment or other non-operating holding. | The same purpose test, other limb. No new book is created by this Manual. |
| A.1 | After “Integrated form” | Issued crypto asset means a crypto asset in respect of which an identifiable issuer, administrator or other person has authority to mint, burn, freeze, recall or halt transfers. Bitcoin is not an issued crypto asset. A fiat-referenced stablecoin is an issued crypto asset and a central-bank-proxy instrument, even when the token carries a private product name. USD-referenced stablecoins, under whatever token names, sit under United States management and control. Reserve practice requires holdings of US Treasuries or equivalent cash under US jurisdictional control. They are not Bitcoin, not sui generis bearer property, and not an adjacent currency for this Manual. | The Manual’s intermediary logic is coherent for issued tokens and for sovereign-unit proxies. It is not coherent for Bitcoin. Naming both the residual class and the proxy character stops a US-Treasury-backed token being read as adjacent currency. |

## A.1 Definitions — qualifications

| Section | Paragraph | Proposed amendments | Motivation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A.1 p. 7 | “Crypto asset” | After the three-limb definition, add: Bitcoin is a crypto asset of a distinct kind and is treated in this Manual as sui generis bearer property and an adjacent currency. Issued-crypto-asset rules, including rules written for fiat-referenced stablecoins, apply to Bitcoin only where this Manual so states. | Preserves their genus. Stops every later “crypto asset” rule applying automatically to Bitcoin. |
| A.1 p. 6 | “Capital” | Inclusion of crypto assets applies to issued crypto assets. Bitcoin constitutes capital only at (a) a realisation as defined above or (b) a transfer between a domestic Authorised CASP and an offshore CASP that changes the beneficial owner or places the unit under an offshore CASP. Self-custody, a domestic self-custody event and a working-medium conversion inside the Republic are not capital-in-transit. A realisation into Rand remains a point at which tax may be due. | “Anything with a monetary value including crypto assets” is the sentence that turns a key movement into an export. Tax attaches at realisation of an investment holding, not at the key. |
| A.1 p. 10 | “Transfer” | A transfer of Bitcoin is either (a) an intermediated transfer through an Authorised CASP or offshore CASP, or (b) a self-custody transfer. Only (a) may constitute an import or export of capital. A working-medium conversion inside the Republic through a domestic Authorised CASP or Authorised Dealer is not an import or export. | “Control regardless of the technology” treats operation of a private key as a capital export. |
| A.1 pp. 7–8 | Export / import of goods or capital | A domestic self-custody event and a working-medium conversion inside the Republic are neither export nor import. An onshore receipt taken up as SA-source business proceeds or as a return of an approved foreign investment is an inward reportable receipt where it passes a CASP or AD. Rand proceeds of that receipt are the ordinary place at which tax may arise. | Recognised homecoming of value is not illicit; it is the moment the fiscus can see the profit. |
| A.1 p. 7 | “Cross-border crypto asset transaction” | Replace “deemed by FinSurv” with: a transfer (a) between a domestic Authorised CASP and an offshore CASP, or (b) that effects a change of beneficial owner across the CMA border. It does not include a domestic self-custody event, a self-custody transfer or a working-medium conversion inside the Republic. | A circular deeming cannot be commented on and cannot give residents notice. |
| A.1 p. 8 | “Non-custodial wallet” | Keep the technical sentence. Add: holding Bitcoin through a non-custodial wallet is self-custody. A transfer involving that wallet is a domestic self-custody event or a self-custody transfer unless an offshore CASP or a change of beneficial owner across the CMA border is present. | The wallet definition should not do the work of the cross-border definition. |

## What this submission is not

- Ask that Bitcoin be declared legal tender, or that the South African Reserve Bank guarantee the unit.
- Ask that POCA, FICA, POCDATARA or FAIS be disapplied.
- Ask Authorised CASPs to stop reporting their offshore-CASP books, or attack the single discretionary allowance or foreign capital allowance as limits on genuine externalisation to an offshore CASP.
- Ask FinSurv to hide a realisation of an investment holding into Rand from SARS. Where Bitcoin presented as an investment becomes Rand at a domestic Authorised CASP or Authorised Dealer, that is the event at which tax may be due. The proposal is that this event happen onshore, on a financial statement, in a reporting module FinSurv already has.
- Treat a fiat-referenced stablecoin as Bitcoin, as sui generis bearer property, or as an adjacent currency. Those tokens are issued crypto assets and central-bank-proxy instruments.
- Reclassify Bitcoin as cash for the purposes of IAS 7, or invent a tax election. Purpose follows the financial statements the resident already keeps.
- Reopen the comments filed with SARS on 31 August 2026 or the objection to the Draft Capital Flow Management Regulations filed on 30 June 2026. Those instruments are cited as context only.
