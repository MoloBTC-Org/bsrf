## 1. Purpose

This document is the jurisdiction overview for South African tax and tax-adjacent treatment of Bitcoin. It does not replace the filings. It maps the same four questions every tax-track nation file answers: who charges, what is the event, which information returns fire, and how capital-flow rules meet the tax base.

Bitcoin is the same protocol in Johannesburg as in Lisbon. The Income Tax Act, the SARS Draft Guide, CARF as implemented by SARS, and the SARB Draft Crypto Asset Manual are the local statutes and drafts.

---

## 2. Charging authority and tax base

Charging sits with the South African Revenue Service under the Income Tax Act.

The BSRF position put to SARS on 31 August 2026 is that mere holding, a self-custody transfer under common control, and personal node operation are not taxable events. Taxation remains coherent at a **realisation** with a sufficient territorial nexus — typically a conversion into Rand (or another fiat) through an observable interface.

That letter is filed. It is not reopened by this overview.

---

## 3. Information reporting

South Africa has incorporated the OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework. Collection of reportable information began 1 March 2026. First returns to SARS are due 31 May 2027. First automatic exchange is scheduled for September 2027.

The reporter is a Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider with a South African nexus. A person in self-custody is not a CARF reporter by reason of holding keys or running a node.

Detail, timelines and schema references: *South_Africa_CARF_Implementation_Status.md* in this folder. International institutional history: *Global_CARF_History_Precedents_and_Governing_Bodies.md*.

---

## 4. Capital-flow interface

Exchange control is administered by SARB Financial Surveillance, not by SARS. The Draft Crypto Asset Manual for Cross-Border Activities (comment deadline 30 September 2026) is the live instrument.

The tax-track ask in that comment is that FinSurv stop treating own-keys as an export, so that a realisation of an investment holding into Rand can occur onshore, on a South African financial statement, where SARS can see it. Purpose follows the financial statements the resident already keeps. No nomination. No IAS 7 reclassification.

Filing pack: `Finsurv Draft Manual/` in this folder.

---

## 5. Self-custody, holding, nodes

The SARS letter and the FinSurv comment use the same stack: self-custody, self-custody transfer, domestic self-custody event. Holding and node operation sit outside both the proposed tax event and the proposed FinSurv transaction.

CARF does not convert those acts into a reporting duty where no Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider is in the path.

---

## 6. Bitcoin versus issued tokens

Bitcoin is defined, in the FinSurv comment, separately from an issued crypto asset. A fiat-referenced stablecoin is an issued crypto asset and a central-bank-proxy instrument. It is not Bitcoin, not *sui generis* bearer property, and not an adjacent currency for that Manual.

The SARS letter asked for the same genus split in the Draft Guide.
