## 1. Purpose

This document records the Spanish tax treatment of Bitcoin and other crypto-assets for BSRF comparative work: who charges, what is a taxable event, which information returns fire, and how self-custody is classified for *foreign-asset* reporting.

Bitcoin is the same protocol in Madrid as in Johannesburg. The local statutes are not. The file exists so those two facts stay separate.

It is a factual companion to *Bitcoin_as_Sui_Generis_Bearer_Property_Tax_Implications_v0.1.md* and to *Global_CARF_History_Precedents_and_Governing_Bodies.md*. It is not Spanish legal advice.

## 2. Charging authority and tax base

Charging sits with the Agencia Estatal de Administración Tributaria under the Impuesto sobre la Renta de las Personas Físicas (IRPF) and, where the taxpayer is in scope, the Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio.

The public DGT line, restated in practitioner notes and in V5066-26 commentary, is that a **realisation into legal tender or into non-crypto assets** is the IRPF event. Mere holding is not that event. Self-custody is not that event. A 721 filing position is not that event.

This file does not treat Spain as a *sui generis* recognition jurisdiction. IRPF still prices a fiat-interface gain. Patrimonio still values a holding that meets its own thresholds.

---

## 3. Information reporting

Spain runs two reporting surfaces that must not be collapsed into one.

**Intermediary reporting (DAC8 / CRS family).** Crypto-asset service providers collect identity and transaction data for automatic exchange. The resident in self-custody is not, by that fact alone, a reporting institution. Contact with a reporting intermediary is the surface.

**Resident foreign-asset return — Modelo 721.** This is an *information* return on crypto-assets held abroad, with a €50,000 threshold measured at 31 December, and a follow-on duty to report loss of title where a prior 721 was already due. It is administered by the tax authority. It is not a customs form and it is not IRPF.

Headline claims that “Spain exempts self-custody” refer to **Modelo 721**, not to IRPF and not to Patrimonio. Recouso’s correction of the 28 September 2026 Bitcoin Archive post is the legally accurate limit.

## 4. Capital-flow interface

This file does not duplicate Spanish exchange-control or Banco de España product rules. Those belong on the law track when a Spain legislative file is opened.

Tax consequence of a capital-flow rule is recorded here only where a Spanish charging or information provision actually turns on it. Modelo 721 is the present example: the form asks whether the asset is *held abroad by a third party*, not whether Bitcoin is legal tender.

---

## 5. Self-custody, holding, nodes

Self-custody — private keys, hardware wallet, paper wallet, or equivalent control in the resident’s hands — is not treated by the DGT as custody by a third party abroad.

Consequences that follow from that classification:

- the holding does not go on Modelo 721;
- it does not count toward the €50,000 721 threshold;
- it does not, by that fact alone, leave IRPF or Patrimonio.

Personal node operation is not a 721 fact. It is not an IRPF event. Ordinary criminal law remains.

---

## 6. Bitcoin versus issued tokens

Nothing in V2290-23 or V5066-26 reclassifies a fiat-referenced stablecoin as Bitcoin, as *sui generis* bearer property, or as an adjacent currency. An issued token held on a foreign platform remains an issued token held on a foreign platform. The 721 geography question (third-party abroad versus own keys) is the same question for both; the charging character is not.

## 7. Current instruments

### 7.1 DGT consulta V2290-23 (2023)

First published administrative confirmation that crypto-assets in autocustodia are not “custodiadas por un tercero en el extranjero” for Modelo 721.

### 7.2 DGT consulta vinculante V5066-26 (June 2026)

Restates V2290-23 on self-custody. Adds the intra-year foreign-exchange limb:

- buy and fully sell on a foreign exchange in the same calendar year → 31 December balance zero → no 721 that year, provided no prior-year 721 duty already exists;
- if a prior year already triggered 721, disposal during the following year still requires a 721 to report extinction of the position.

The form looks at the 31 December balance and at whether an obligation previously existed. It does not look at whether the taxpayer touched crypto during the year.

### 7.3 What V5066-26 does not do

It does not:

- declare Bitcoin legal tender;
- remove IRPF from a realisation into euros or into non-crypto assets;
- remove Patrimonio from an in-scope holding, including a self-custodied holding;
- create a South African nomination, a Portuguese 365-day clock, or a FinSurv “working-medium” rule.
