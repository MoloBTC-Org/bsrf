## Executive Summary

Mauritius operates a dedicated virtual-asset licensing regime under the **Virtual Asset and Initial Token Offering Services (VAITOS) Act 2021**, in force from **February 2022**, supervised by the **Financial Services Commission**. It is the regional holding-structure jurisdiction most often named by East and Southern African operators when local capital floors rise (notably Kenya’s 2026 regulations).

Licence classes include broker-dealer (Class M), wallet services (Class O) and custodian (Class R), among others. Licensees must be directed and managed from Mauritius. Self-custody by individuals is not the licensed activity. Bitcoin is not legal tender.

## 1. Historical Thread

- VAITOS Act passed 2021; commenced February 2022.
- Transitional window for pre-existing operators of 3–18 months from commencement.
- 2026: continued FSC licensing (including multi-class grants such as broker-dealer, wallet and custodian).

## 2. Current Regulatory Overview

### Licensing
No person may carry on VASP or ITO business in or from Mauritius without an FSC licence or registration under VAITOS. Physical presence, client-asset segregation and audited accounts are standard expectations.

### Classification
Virtual assets are a regulated non-bank financial-services activity. They are not Mauritian legal tender.

### Self-custody
The Act licences businesses. Private key possession by a natural person is not a VAITOS class.

## 3. Key Players

## 4. Platforms for Advocacy

## 5. Philosophical & Legal Synthesis

VAITOS is a mature intermediary statute. African firms use it as a regional base. That fact is about corporate geography, not about Bitcoin’s legal nature. The Framework still requires a separation between licensed custody/exchange and self-custody.

## 6. Adoption Implications

- A Mauritian VASP licence does not passport into Kenya, Nigeria or South Africa.
- Individuals in Mauritius may hold keys without becoming Class R custodians.

## Truth & Clarity Summary

Mauritius has a working VASP statute and an active FSC licence book. It is an intermediary jurisdiction. It is not a Bitcoin legal-tender jurisdiction and it does not prohibit self-custody.
