## Executive Summary

Botswana regulates the sector under the **Virtual Assets Act, 2025 (Act 4 of 2025)**, commenced **24 January 2025**, read with S.I. 9 of 2025. Consolidated text: [botswanalaws.com record](https://botswanalaws.com/consolidated-statutes/principle-legislation/virtual-assets-act). The Regulatory Authority is the **Non-Bank Financial Institutions Regulatory Authority (NBFIRA)**.

The Act licences VASPs and issuers of initial token offerings carrying on business into or from Botswana, wherever the activity is physically performed. Virtual assets are defined to exclude legal tender and securities already regulated under the Securities Act. NBFIRA publishes a list of licensed VASPs. Self-custody by individuals is not the licensed activity.

## 1. Historical Thread

- Virtual Assets Act 2022, later repealed and replaced by the 2025 Act.
- VASPs designated as non-bank financial institutions under the Financial Intelligence Act.
- **24 January 2025**: 2025 Act commences.
- March 2025 NBFIRA AML/CFT guidance for VA businesses.
- NBFIRA public register lists licensed VASPs (including Yellow Card Botswana and others).

## 2. Current Regulatory Overview

### Licensing
NBFIRA licences exchange, transfer, custody and issuance-related services. The Act is declared a financial-services law for NBFIRA purposes.

### Classification
The statutory definition of “virtual asset” excludes digital representations of legal tender under the Bank of Botswana Act and excludes securities regulated under the Securities Act. That is a perimeter definition, not a *sui generis* Bitcoin statute.

### Self-custody
The Act applies to service providers and ITO issuers. Private holding is outside that application clause.

## 3. Key Players

- NBFIRA
- Financial Intelligence Agency (Financial Intelligence Act)
- Bank of Botswana (legal-tender boundary)

## 4. Platforms for Advocacy

- NBFIRA licensing and the published VASP register.
- Any amendment regulations under sections 42–43 of the Act.

## 5. Philosophical & Legal Synthesis

Botswana’s definition already separates virtual assets from legal tender and from Securities Act instruments. That is closer to a distinct-class approach than Nigeria’s “everything is a security” model. It still licences *services*, not keys.

## 6. Adoption Implications

- Licensed exchanges exist and are named on the NBFIRA register.
- Unlicensed service provision into Botswana is within the Act even if the servers sit elsewhere.

## Next Steps

1. Refresh the NBFIRA register when names change.
2. Watch whether Bank of Botswana issues a payments view on Bitcoin.
3. Note any 2026 amendment Act.

## Truth & Clarity Summary

Botswana has an in-force Virtual Assets Act and a live NBFIRA licence list. Self-custody is not the licensed act. Bitcoin is a virtual asset under that Act, not legal tender.
