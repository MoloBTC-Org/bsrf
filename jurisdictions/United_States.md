## Executive Summary

The United States remains a fragmented but relatively permissive jurisdiction for Bitcoin. Bitcoin is treated as property by the IRS and is widely regarded as a commodity rather than a security. There is no federal wealth tax or unrealized-gains tax on crypto holdings. Self-custody is generally protected and is not subject to broad federal reporting requirements solely for holding.

The most significant live legislative vehicle is the **Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (CLARITY Act, H.R. 3633)**. Publicly framed as delivering regulatory clarity by allocating jurisdiction between the SEC and CFTC and reducing enforcement uncertainty, successive drafts have also constructed a new market-structure architecture with registration, definitional, and intermediary obligations. Recent drafts contain meaningful protective language for non-custodial developers (Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act / BRCA) and for self-custody (Keep Your Coins provisions and language shielding dormant self-custodied assets from pure inactivity-based abandonment claims). As of early August 2026 the bill has not received a Senate floor vote and remains short of the 60 votes required for cloture.

A temporary statutory restriction on Federal Reserve issuance of a retail CBDC (through 31 December 2030) further reinforces the distinction between Bitcoin and centrally issued digital currency.

## 1. Historical Thread

- **18 March 2013**: FinCEN FIN-2013-G001 — users of convertible virtual currency are not MSBs; administrators and exchangers generally are money transmitters. A person who creates units of a decentralised convertible virtual currency (including by mining) and uses them to purchase goods or services is a user, not a money transmitter. Official text: https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/guidance/application-fincens-regulations-persons-administering
- **2014**: IRS Notice 2014-21 treating virtual currency as property.
- **2015–2023**: Parallel SEC and CFTC enforcement and guidance; growing industry demand for statutory market-structure clarity.
- **2024–2025**: FIT21 passes the House; reintroduced and advanced elements later appear in the CLARITY Act.
- **July 2025**: House passes the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (H.R. 3633) by a wide bipartisan margin (294–134).
- **May 2026**: Senate Banking Committee advances its version 15–9.
- **June–July 2026**: Merged Senate text released; bill placed on the legislative calendar but no floor vote held before the August recess.
- **June 2026**: Temporary prohibition on Federal Reserve retail CBDC issuance enacted through related legislation (effective until 31 December 2030).

## 2. Current Regulatory Overview

### Classification and Taxation
- IRS treats Bitcoin as **property**. Gains are taxed on realization.
- No federal tax on unrealized gains and no federal wealth tax on crypto holdings.
- CFTC has long treated Bitcoin as a commodity; the CLARITY Act seeks to place spot digital-commodity markets primarily under CFTC oversight while preserving SEC authority over securities.

### Self-Custody
Self-custody is generally protected. There is no broad federal requirement to report mere holdings of self-custodied Bitcoin (FBAR and other foreign-account rules may still apply in specific cross-border situations). Recent CLARITY drafts contain explicit language (Keep Your Coins / related provisions) stating that federal agencies may not prohibit or impair the ability to self-custody, and additional language intended to prevent self-custodied assets from being treated as abandoned solely on the basis of inactivity.

### Market Structure Legislation – The Clarity Problem
The CLARITY Act is publicly justified as the solution to regulatory uncertainty: clearer jurisdictional lines, reduced reliance on enforcement actions, and a workable framework for digital commodity markets.

In substance, the bill does more than allocate existing authority. Successive drafts create or expand:

- Definitions of digital commodities, trading systems, and related categories;
- Registration and intermediary obligations;
- Certification and stay mechanisms;
- Rules around “control” and “mature” blockchain systems.

This produces a structural tension. The political message is clarity and reduced uncertainty. The operative text constructs a new regulatory architecture. Whether the net effect is genuine clarification or scope expansion under the banner of clarity is the central analytical question the framework must confront honestly.

Protective provisions in recent drafts are real and valuable:
- Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act (BRCA) language shielding non-custodial software developers from money-transmitter status solely for publishing code or providing self-custody tools;
- Self-custody protections;
- Attempts to pre-empt pure inactivity-based abandonment claims against self-custodied assets.

These protections coexist with the broader market-structure machinery. Both must be recorded accurately.

**Status (3 September 2026)**: House-passed (July 2025, 294–134); Senate Banking advanced 15–9 (May 2026); merged text exists; **no Senate floor vote and no cloture motion as of early September 2026**. The August recess closed without a vote. A short September window remains before midterm campaigning. The 60-vote cloture threshold is unmet.

### CBDC
A temporary statutory restriction prevents the Federal Reserve from issuing a retail CBDC until 31 December 2030. This does not apply to stablecoins and does not affect Bitcoin.

## 5. Philosophical & Legal Synthesis

The United States presents the sharpest version of the “clarity versus scope” problem. Because the political system responds to demands for legal certainty, legislation framed as clarifying existing authority readily becomes the vehicle for defining new categories and imposing new obligations.

From the framework’s perspective the critical distinctions remain:

- Bitcoin as sui generis bearer property controlled by private key, not an account relationship;
- Self-custody as the exercise of property and privacy rights rather than a licensed financial activity;
- Publication and operation of non-custodial software as closer to speech and engineering than to intermediation.

Where statutory language protects those distinctions (BRCA, Keep Your Coins, inactivity shields), it advances the framework’s positions. Where it recharacterises pure cryptographic control or publication as regulated market activity, it expands the perimeter under the language of clarity. Both dynamics are present in the CLARITY trajectory and must be tracked without softening either.

Constitutional baselines (Fifth and Fourteenth Amendment property protections, First Amendment considerations for code and publication, and the limits on federal power) remain the ultimate backstop.

## 6. Adoption Implications

- Self-custody remains legally viable and relatively well protected compared with many jurisdictions.
- The outcome of the CLARITY Act will determine whether the United States moves toward a clearer commodity-market framework with meaningful non-custodial carve-outs, or toward a more comprehensive licensing regime that increases the compliance surface for intermediaries and adjacent activity.
- The temporary retail CBDC restriction reduces one vector of direct monetary competition and surveillance concern through 2030.
- The United States continues to function as both the largest market and the most important source of legal precedent for the global treatment of Bitcoin.

**Truth & Clarity Summary**  
The United States offers a relatively permissive baseline for Bitcoin as property and for self-custody. The CLARITY Act is the primary live attempt to replace enforcement uncertainty with statute. It contains both valuable protective provisions for non-custodial activity and significant new market-structure machinery. Treating “clarity” as an unexamined good without examining actual scope is inconsistent with the standard of this framework. Both the protections and the expansions must be recorded with equal precision.
