## Executive Summary

Kazakhstan’s primary legislative framework for digital assets rests on the **Law on Digital Assets in the Republic of Kazakhstan** (Law No. 193-VII of 6 February 2023, as amended). From **1 May 2026**, a comprehensive regulatory regime for the circulation of digital assets came into force, introducing licensing for unsecured digital asset exchange operators and registration requirements for trading platforms, under the oversight of the National Bank of Kazakhstan.

On **7 July 2026**, President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev signed a decree “On measures to stimulate and develop the digital assets industry.” The decree directs the creation of incentives to bring activity into the regulated domestic framework, including a planned multi-year personal income tax exemption for individuals on gains realised through licensed Kazakh providers (2026–2028), mechanisms for the use of digital assets and stablecoins in cross-border settlements, and a voluntary disclosure pathway for assets previously held on unregulated foreign platforms.

A dedicated Committee on Digital Assets and Payment Systems is being established under the National Bank.

Self-custody of Bitcoin by individuals is not prohibited. The legislative emphasis is on channelling trading and service provision through licensed domestic infrastructure while offering tax and regulatory incentives to do so.

Industrial mining, preferential power, and the National Strategic Crypto Reserve are treated in the companion file `Strategic_Mining.md` in this folder. They are not part of this legislative note.

## 1. Foundational Legislation

**Law on Digital Assets in the Republic of Kazakhstan (No. 193-VII, 6 February 2023)**

This is the core statute. It distinguishes:
- Unsecured digital assets (cryptoassets)
- Digital financial assets (including stablecoins, tokenized real assets, and digital forms of traditional instruments)

The law provides the legal basis for issuance, circulation, and the licensing of digital asset service providers. Digital mining is expressly permitted for individual entrepreneurs and legal entities and is stated not to constitute the organisation of digital asset turnover.

Amendments effective from 1 May 2026 strengthened the licensing and registration regime for market participants.

---

## 2. July 2026 Presidential Decree

**Decree “On measures to stimulate and develop the digital assets industry in the Republic of Kazakhstan” (7 July 2026)**

Key directions include:

- Development of mechanisms enabling the use of digital assets and stablecoins in cross-border settlements for export and import operations.
- Planned personal income tax exemption for individuals on income from digital asset transactions conducted through Kazakhstan’s regulated infrastructure (draft law to be submitted by 1 April 2027, covering the period 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2028).
- Legal mechanism for voluntary disclosure of previously acquired digital assets, conditional on their transfer into the regulated domestic infrastructure via licensed Kazakh service providers (to be established by 31 December 2026).
- Additional tax incentives to encourage the transfer of assets from foreign platforms to domestic providers (by 1 September 2026).
- Establishment of a Committee on Digital Assets and Payment Systems under the National Bank.
- Development of tokenized financial instruments, including potential tokenized government securities.

The decree was prepared jointly by the Ministry of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Development, the National Bank of Kazakhstan, and the Astana International Financial Centre.

## 3. Licensing and Market Structure

From 1 May 2026:
- Operators of unsecured digital asset exchanges require a licence from the National Bank.
- Operators of digital asset trading platforms and digital financial asset platforms are subject to registration.

Customer asset segregation rules and integration of licensed platforms with second-tier banks have been progressive priorities. The framework aims to move activity out of the unregulated/offshore sector into a supervised domestic environment.

---

## 4. Implications for Bitcoin and Self-Custody

- **Self-custody**: Individual self-custody of Bitcoin is not prohibited by the current legislative framework. Restrictions and licensing obligations apply primarily to service providers and organised trading platforms.
- **Tax treatment**: The planned multi-year personal income tax exemption is limited to transactions conducted through regulated Kazakh providers. This creates a clear incentive to use licensed domestic infrastructure.
- **Cross-border utility**: Explicit policy support for stablecoins and digital assets in foreign trade settlements expands the practical use cases beyond pure speculation.
- **Sui generis considerations**: By creating a dedicated statutory category for digital assets (unsecured cryptoassets distinct from traditional financial instruments) and a specialised regulatory committee, Kazakhstan treats Bitcoin and similar assets as a distinct class rather than forcing them into pre-existing securities or banking categories.

## 5. Monitoring Points

- Final text and parliamentary passage of the personal income tax exemption legislation (deadline April 2027).
- Operational launch and mandate of the Committee on Digital Assets and Payment Systems.
- Implementation of the voluntary disclosure and asset transfer mechanism.
- Any further amendments to the Law on Digital Assets affecting self-custody reporting or capital controls.
- Interaction between the legislative framework and the separate strategic mining regime (see companion document).

**Truth & Clarity Summary**  
Kazakhstan’s legislative framework is built on the 2023 Law on Digital Assets, operationalised through a May 2026 licensing regime and significantly expanded by the July 2026 presidential decree. The policy direction prioritises bringing activity into a regulated domestic ecosystem via tax incentives, stablecoin settlement channels, and voluntary disclosure pathways. Self-custody remains outside the primary licensing perimeter.
