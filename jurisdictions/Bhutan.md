# Bhutan – Legislative Framework

**Bitcoin Regulation, Self-Custody & Sui Generis Treatment**

## Executive Summary

Bhutan does not have a comprehensive national cryptocurrency statute. Regulation is characterised by a dual structure:

- **Mainland Bhutan**, supervised by the Royal Monetary Authority (RMA), maintains a restrictive stance. Crypto mining and exchange activities are confined to entities operating under the Gelephu Mindfulness City (GMC) framework. On-ramping through domestic banks is restricted.
- **Gelephu Mindfulness City (GMC)**, a special administrative region, operates under its own regulatory architecture (Financial Services Office). It has introduced licensing for digital asset firms, including a fast-track pathway for entities already regulated in recognised jurisdictions (Singapore, Abu Dhabi Global Market, Hong Kong, etc.), and offers significant tax incentives.

Self-custody of Bitcoin by individuals is not expressly prohibited, but the practical ability to acquire, trade, or convert Bitcoin through regulated domestic channels outside GMC remains highly constrained.

State mining through Druk Holding & Investments, sovereign holdings, and the Gelephu Mindfulness City Bitcoin allocation are treated in the companion file `Strategic_Mining_Sovereign_Holdings.md` in this folder. They are not part of this legislative note.

## 1. Regulatory Structure

### Royal Monetary Authority (RMA) Stance

In 2025 the RMA formalised its position (notably the April/May 2025 communications on its regulatory stance regarding cryptocurrency):

- Cryptocurrency mining operations and exchange services are permitted **only** for entities registered within and regulated by Gelephu Mindfulness City.
- Activities outside GMC remain restricted.
- On-ramping (conversion of Ngultrum into crypto assets via domestic RMA-regulated banks) is not authorised for the general public.

This creates a ring-fenced approach: innovation and commercial activity are channelled into the special administrative region while the broader financial system is shielded from retail crypto exposure.

### Gelephu Mindfulness City Framework

GMC functions as a special administrative region with a high degree of autonomy in financial services regulation. Key features include:

- Licensing of virtual asset service providers (exchanges, custodians, trading platforms) through the Gelephu Financial Services Office.
- Fast-track / accelerated licensing pathway for firms already supervised in recognised financial centres.
- Integration of licensing with banking access.
- Attractive tax regime for qualifying entities (including potential 0% corporate tax for priority sectors subject to substance requirements, territorial taxation principles, and exemptions from certain capital gains, dividend and inheritance taxes through 2030 in specified cases).

GMC is positioning itself as a controlled digital-asset and fintech hub rather than a fully open national regime.

---

## 2. Tax Treatment

There is no comprehensive public national guidance equivalent to a detailed crypto tax code. Within GMC, the incentive structure is designed to attract institutional and commercial digital-asset activity. Outside GMC, ordinary tax principles apply and retail crypto activity remains limited by the RMA’s restrictions on on-ramping and service provision.

---

## 3. Implications for Bitcoin and Self-Custody

- **Self-custody**: Individual holding of Bitcoin is not prohibited. The primary constraints concern regulated acquisition, trading, and conversion channels.
- **Geographic limitation**: Commercial mining and exchange activity is deliberately concentrated inside GMC.
- **Sui generis considerations**: Bhutan has not attempted to force Bitcoin into existing securities or banking categories at the national level. Instead, it has created a specialised jurisdictional space (GMC) in which digital assets can be treated under a dedicated regulatory regime. This is consistent with recognising Bitcoin’s distinct character while containing systemic risk to the broader economy.

---

## 4. Monitoring Points

- Further development of GMC’s licensing practice and the number of firms authorised.
- Any expansion or relaxation of RMA restrictions on mainland activity.
- Clarification of tax treatment for individuals holding or disposing of Bitcoin outside GMC.
- Interaction between GMC rules and national AML/CFT requirements.
- Evolution of the dual “two systems” model (GMC versus mainland Bhutan).

---

**Truth & Clarity Summary**  
Bhutan’s legislative approach is dual-track: restrictive at the national level under the RMA, and deliberately open within the ring-fenced special administrative region of Gelephu Mindfulness City. There is no comprehensive national crypto statute. Self-custody remains possible, but regulated on-ramps and commercial activity are channelled into GMC.
