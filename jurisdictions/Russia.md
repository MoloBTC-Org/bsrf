## Executive Summary

Russia’s first comprehensive cryptocurrency statute is now in force. Bill № 1194918-8, “On Digital Currency and Digital Rights,” passed the State Duma on **21 July 2026**, was signed by President Putin on **4 August 2026** as **Federal Law No. 282-FZ**, and **took effect on 1 September 2026**. A transition period for certain market participants runs until **1 July 2027**.

The law creates a Bank of Russia-supervised framework for licensed intermediaries. Cryptocurrency is recognised as **property**. Domestic use as a means of payment remains banned. Cross-border settlements in crypto are expressly permitted for companies. Non-qualified retail investors may purchase only through licensed intermediaries, subject to an annual cap of approximately 300,000 rubles per intermediary.

Self-custody is not prohibited. Access to organised markets is heavily intermediated.

- Crypto treated as property for tax in some contexts; domestic payments prohibited.
- Mining regulated and, in periods, used for export revenue.

- Sanctions after 2022 accelerated official interest in crypto as a foreign-trade rail outside SWIFT.

## 2. Current Regulatory Overview

- **Property status**: Cryptocurrency recognised as property under Russian civil law.
- **Licensing**: Bank of Russia licenses and supervises exchanges, brokers, management companies, depositories and related entities. Only registered entities may lawfully operate organised markets.
- **Domestic payments**: Prohibited. The ruble remains sole legal tender for domestic goods and services. Advertising crypto as a means of payment is restricted.
- **Cross-border settlements**: Permitted for companies — the primary strategic purpose of the statute.
- **Retail limits**: Non-qualified investors — licensed intermediaries only; annual cap of approximately 300,000 rubles. Qualified investors — no such quantitative cap.
- **Asset restrictions**: Privacy coins barred from organised trading. Initially, the most liquid assets (primarily Bitcoin and Ethereum) are expected to qualify.
- **Digital ruble**: A retail CBDC programme proceeded on a separate track and should not be confused with the 282-FZ market statute.

## 5. Philosophical & Legal Synthesis

Russia treats digital assets as a distinct statutory class (property plus a specialised market law) rather than folding them into ordinary securities statutes. That classification is consistent with the Framework’s insistence that Bitcoin is not an ordinary account product. The overall philosophy is the opposite of liberal self-custody policy: domestic payment is banned, retail access is capped, and the useful rail is a state-supervised cross-border channel.

Self-custody remains the boundary the statute does not abolish. Enforcement will concentrate on licensed intermediation and domestic payment, not on the existence of a key.

---

## 6. Adoption Implications

- Holding Bitcoin privately remains possible.
- Using Bitcoin as domestic cash is unlawful.
- Companies may find a formal, supervised path for foreign-trade settlement.
- Ordinary retail access to organised venues is quantitatively capped.

**Truth & Clarity Summary**  
Federal Law 282-FZ is in force from 1 September 2026. It legalises regulated trading and corporate cross-border settlement under the Bank of Russia, keeps the domestic payment ban, and caps ordinary retail purchases through licensed intermediaries. Self-custody is not prohibited. The driver is geopolitical control of a settlement rail, not financial liberalisation.
