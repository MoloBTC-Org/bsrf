## Executive Summary

On **21 July 2026**, Russia’s State Duma passed bill № 1194918-8, titled **“On Digital Currency and Digital Rights”** (О цифровых валютах и цифровых правах), in its second and third readings. This is Russia’s first comprehensive cryptocurrency law.

The legislation creates a tightly controlled, Bank of Russia-supervised framework for licensed crypto market participants while explicitly permitting cryptocurrency for **cross-border settlements**. Domestic use of crypto as a means of payment remains banned. Non-qualified retail investors face an annual purchase cap of approximately 300,000 rubles (~US$3,800) through licensed intermediaries.

The law is currently awaiting Federation Council approval and President Putin’s signature. Most provisions are scheduled to take effect on **1 September 2026**, with a transition period running until **1 July 2027**.

---

## 1. Historical Context

Russia has long maintained a restrictive stance on cryptocurrency:

- Crypto has been treated as property for tax purposes in certain contexts, but domestic payments have been prohibited.
- Mining has been regulated and, in some periods, encouraged for export revenue.
- Successive Central Bank and Ministry of Finance proposals oscillated between outright bans and controlled legalisation.
- Western sanctions after 2022 accelerated interest in crypto as a tool for foreign trade settlements outside the SWIFT system.

The 2026 law represents the culmination of several years of debate and is explicitly designed to create legal channels for international settlements while preserving tight domestic control.

## 2. The Law: Key Provisions

**Bill details**
- Full title: Federal Law “On Digital Currency and Digital Rights”
- Number: 1194918-8
- Passed by State Duma (second and third readings): 21 July 2026
- Official tracker: [https://sozd.duma.gov.ru/bill/1194918-8](https://sozd.duma.gov.ru/bill/1194918-8)

**Core elements**

- **Property status**: Cryptocurrency is recognised as property under Russian civil law.
- **Licensing regime**: The Bank of Russia will license and supervise exchanges, brokers, management companies, depositories, and related entities. Only entities in a special registry may lawfully operate.
- **Domestic payments**: Strictly prohibited. The ruble remains the sole legal tender for goods and services inside Russia. Advertising crypto as a means of payment is also restricted.
- **Cross-border settlements**: Explicitly permitted for companies. This is the primary strategic purpose of the legislation — providing a state-supervised channel for foreign trade under sanctions.
- **Retail investor limits**: Non-qualified investors may purchase cryptocurrency only through licensed intermediaries and are subject to an annual cap of approximately 300,000 rubles (~US$3,800). Qualified investors face no such quantitative limit.
- **Asset restrictions**: Privacy coins that obscure transaction data are barred from organised trading. Initially, only the most liquid assets (primarily Bitcoin and Ethereum) are expected to qualify for organised trading.
- **Transition period**: Most rules take effect 1 September 2026; market participants have until 1 July 2027 to fully comply.

## 3. Implications for Bitcoin and Self-Custody

- **Self-custody**: The law does not prohibit individuals from holding Bitcoin in self-custody. The restrictions focus on licensed intermediaries, organised trading, and the use of crypto as a means of domestic payment.
- **Trading access**: Retail access to crypto is channelled through licensed, Central Bank-supervised entities and is quantitatively capped for non-qualified investors.
- **Cross-border utility**: The explicit legalisation of crypto for foreign trade settlements is the most significant innovation. This creates a formal, state-recognised pathway for Bitcoin and other digital assets in international commerce under sanctions pressure.
- **Sui generis considerations**: By recognising cryptocurrency as property while imposing a specialised licensing and settlement regime, Russia is treating digital assets as a distinct category rather than forcing them into existing securities or banking law. This is consistent with the framework’s emphasis on Bitcoin’s unique characteristics, even if the overall regulatory philosophy remains highly centralised and state-directed.

---

## 4. Strategic and Geopolitical Context

The legislation is widely understood as a response to Western sanctions and the exclusion of major Russian banks from SWIFT. By creating a regulated channel for crypto-based cross-border settlements, the Russian state is attempting to formalise an alternative payment rail while retaining supervisory control through the Bank of Russia.

For the broader Bitcoin ecosystem, the law illustrates a dual approach common in sanctioned or high-control jurisdictions: strict domestic restrictions paired with pragmatic international use cases.

---

## 5. Next Steps and Monitoring Points

- Federation Council approval and presidential signature (expected within approximately two weeks of Duma passage).
- Issuance of subordinate regulations by the Bank of Russia defining licensing criteria, capital requirements, and operational standards.
- Practical implementation of the cross-border settlement mechanism.
- Enforcement posture toward pure self-custody and peer-to-peer activity that remains outside licensed channels.
- Any subsequent amendments affecting privacy coins, mining, or qualified investor thresholds.

---

**Truth & Clarity Summary**  
Russia’s July 2026 law is a landmark development: the country’s first comprehensive crypto framework. It legalises regulated trading under tight Central Bank control, maintains the domestic payment ban, and creates an explicit legal pathway for crypto in foreign trade settlements. Self-custody of Bitcoin is not prohibited, but access to markets is heavily intermediated and capped for ordinary retail investors. The primary driver is geopolitical — sanctions circumvention through state-supervised channels — rather than broad financial liberalisation.
